# BAU e-Advising Rules Review

Reviewed on 4 October 2026 against the supplied checklist and BAU's published undergraduate information. This file separates published rules from department choices so the portal does not present an estimate as an official decision.

## Corrected in the portal

- Academic probation now requires **both** the semester GPA and CGPA to be below 2.00.
- The remedial stage now follows two consecutive academic warnings, rather than two low CGPAs alone.
- The graduating load ceiling is 21 credits, not 22.
- CMPS006 (AI for All) is marked as a Computer Science course, so it does not fill a CS university-elective requirement.
- A plan or registration now warns when consecutive classes on different campuses leave less than 60 minutes for travel. This is department advising guidance, not a BAU registration block.
- General Science Electives normally use a 3-credit and a 2-credit course. A 3+3 combination may be used when no suitable 2-credit course is offered or fits.
- Old "not seen" wording was merged into the clearer "Not registered" notice. The last available record is still shown.

## Confirmed published rules

- Fall and spring registration is normally 12–18 credits; summer is capped at 9.
- A student with at least a 3.33 CGPA and 34 earned credits may take up to 21 credits with the required approval.
- A student in the graduation year may take up to 21 credits when both CGPA and semester GPA are at least 2.00, with advisor and dean approval.
- Graduating students and students under academic warning may be allowed below 12 credits in fall or spring.
- IC3 must be completed during the first four semesters and carries no degree credit.
- A degree-plan form is submitted before the graduation semester.
- Exam re-correction petitions are filed within one week of results, for no more than two courses, with the required fee.
- Attendance warnings are issued at 10% and 15% absence; absence above 20% leads to AW.
- Transfer credit requires an approved equivalent, at least a C grade, and cannot exceed 50% of the programme.

## Department confirmation still needed

These points are not clear enough in the published material to hard-code as official rules:

- Which catalogue applies at the 2025/2026 boundary, especially for WRNL200.
- Whether an extra MATH course may fill a departmental elective, and which courses qualify.
- Which low-grade courses must be repeated during the remedial stage; the current portal leaves the final choice to the advisor.
- Whether three departmental electives are always required and how an official track choice is recorded. The current track label is deliberately an estimate unless a track is recorded.
- The exact allowed companion courses and load for each Intensive English level.
- Whether MATH246, MATH346, or both may count toward the Mathematics minor in every catalogue.
- A live source for section capacity and closed-section status.

## Recommended next features

1. **Requests and forms hub** — one student timeline for Registration, Drop/Add, Automatic Withdrawal, Course Withdrawal, Change of Major, Incomplete Grade, Student Petition, Leave of Absence, Academic Warning, Attendance Warning and Grade Change requests. Show status, owner, deadline and attachments.
2. **Graduation workflow** — degree-plan form, remaining requirements, approvals and clearance checklist in one place.
3. **Petition deadline tracker** — highlight the one-week exam re-correction deadline and the two-course limit.
4. **Attendance warnings** — record 10%, 15% and 20% thresholds and prepare the correct warning or AW action.
5. **Live offering status** — show when sections were last refreshed, remaining seats and newly closed sections.
6. **Advisor approvals queue** — collect overload, underload, prerequisite exception and graduation-load decisions with an audit trail.
7. **Transfer-equivalency workspace** — store course descriptions, department decisions and reusable equivalency mappings without counting more than 50% of the degree.
8. **Leave and re-enrolment workflow** — track one- or two-semester leave, longer interruption and the required return approval.
9. **Change-of-major preview** — show which completed courses will still count before a student submits the form.
10. **Rule evidence panel** — show the official source, last verified date and whether each rule is official, a department setting or an advising estimate.

The first three features would give Dr. Julie the most immediate value because they turn BAU's existing forms and deadlines into a visible, defensible advising process.
